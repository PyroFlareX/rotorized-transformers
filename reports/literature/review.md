# Literature review

This review records methods relevant to structured projection compression. Parameter counts are stated only at the representation-family level where supported; exact counts depend on dimensions, factorization depth, block sizes, and constraints. “Index-free” means the structural pairing/layout is generated from fixed rules rather than serialized per coefficient; it does not imply zero metadata or zero implementation overhead. None of the cited works demonstrates quality or speed for this project's specific rotor/scaler cascade. Bibliographic entries are included only where primary-source metadata were checked; other leads are in **To verify**.

## Rotation-based neural networks: KAMA-NNs
KAMA-NNs parameterize feed-forward layers using products of learned or random low-dimensional rotations. The paper discusses space-efficient rotation products and comparison with unstructured layers. Its count scales with the number of rotation parameters and layers/factors; it is not a single universal count and is not reproduced here. Pair layouts are structured, although the precise storage/runtime contract differs from this project's topology-generated Givens pairs. This is the closest conceptual precedent for learned rotation products, but it does not by itself establish that a full-width Transformer matrix benefits from the current diagonal-scaled cascade. Equal-byte truncated-SVD comparisons remain necessary to separate rotation structure from rank reduction. Verified source: \citep{choromanski2019kama}.

## ACDC and related structured linear layers
ACDC (“A Structured Efficient Linear Layer”) is a structured efficient layer approach and a relevant comparator for replacing dense linear maps. Its structure and factor parameterization differ from the cascade's fixed topology of disjoint Givens pairs plus learned diagonal scales. A fixed structured pattern can avoid storing arbitrary indices, but the number of learned parameters and any structural metadata must be counted for the chosen dimensions. The bibliographic page was located, but exact author/venue/year metadata have not been fully checked against the primary source for this review; do not cite it in the paper bibliography yet. See **To verify**.

## Butterfly factorization and Pixelated Butterfly
Butterfly factorization represents transforms using products of sparse staged factors, with block interactions arranged by a butterfly pattern. In the cited learned-algorithm paper, each factor is a sparse butterfly structure; parameter count depends on depth and the degrees of freedom in each block. The regular pattern is index-free under known dimensions and convention. The project's “butterfly” topology is specifically a schedule of Givens-pair stages, not the unrestricted 2-by-2 block butterfly parameterization, so these should not be conflated. Pixelated Butterfly is a related structured-family lead but its bibliographic details and exact comparison scope have not been verified here. These methods provide structural baselines, not proof that any observed benefit is attributable to rotations rather than differing parameter counts or rank limits. Verified butterfly source: \citep{dao2019butterfly}.

## Monarch
Monarch matrices are products of two block-diagonal matrices, designed as expressive, hardware-efficient structured matrices. Their parameter count is determined by the block sizes and matrix shape; the fixed block layout is reconstructible from dimensions and design settings, rather than arbitrary per-entry indices. Unlike the cascade, Monarch does not constrain each local block to be a rotation and does not use the cascade's learned diagonal scalers between multiple rotor products. It offers an important alternative structured baseline. As with all such comparisons, equal stored bytes are needed to distinguish a structural advantage from lower rank or a different parameter budget. Verified source: \citep{dao2022monarch}.

## Other structured matrices: Toeplitz/circulant and displacement rank
Toeplitz/circulant maps tie coefficients along diagonals or cyclic diagonals, yielding parameter counts proportional to the number of independent diagonals (or the vector length for a circulant matrix), rather than the dense matrix product of dimensions. Their regular index structure is implicit. Low-displacement-rank approaches parameterize a matrix through a small displacement generator; generator size, displacement operator, and representation details determine the count, and indexing is typically rule-based. These families differ sharply from products of orthogonal rotations: they impose algebraic structure, not necessarily orthogonality. They can be compared with the cascade at equal bytes but are not SVD baselines. Primary-source bibliographic details for specific representative works are **To verify**.

## Orthogonal/unitary parameterizations: EUNN, Givens, Householder, Cayley
EUNN gives a tunable parameterization of unitary transformations for recurrent networks. Its degrees of freedom depend on the chosen unitary subspace/architecture; it is not directly a claim about compressing arbitrary rectangular FFN matrices. Givens rotations encode orthogonal transformations with one angle per plane rotation in a specified sequence. Householder products use one vector per reflection (with normalization/scale conventions affecting parameter count); Cayley maps use a skew-symmetric parameter and a matrix transform, with parameterization costs and numerical/runtime properties distinct from staged pairwise rotations. Orthogonal/unitary parameterizations can be index-free given a fixed sequence or deterministic rule, but orthogonality alone does not guarantee fewer bytes than dense weights or a better approximation. SVD--Givens is especially important here: after truncating to rank $q$, an $m\times q$ Stiefel factor has $qm-q(q+1)/2$ degrees of freedom. The rank truncation supplies the principal reduction; the Stiefel constraint accounts for the smaller reduction in factor degrees of freedom. EUNN metadata verified in primary source: \citep{jing2017eunn}. Individual canonical Householder and Cayley references are **To verify**.

## Low-rank LLM compression and LoRA-style residuals
Truncated SVD represents an $m\times p$ matrix as two low-rank factors with approximately $q(m+p)$ coefficients (plus singular-value placement/convention); rank $q$ is the central capacity control. SVD-LLM is a truncation-aware SVD approach to LLM compression, while ASVD uses activation-aware scaling/weighting around singular-value decomposition. These methods explicitly exploit low rank or rank truncation and therefore form necessary comparators when evaluating the cascade. A rotor/scaler cascade is full-rank-capable, but with no residual its approximation performance must be compared at the same actual stored-byte budget. Adding a rank-$r$ residual uses $r(m+p)$ coefficients in the current implementation. This is conceptually analogous to adding a low-rank correction, but should not be described as LoRA fine-tuning: the fitting setup and training objective can differ. SVD-LLM and ASVD primary arXiv records: \citep{wang2024svdllm,yuan2023asvd}.

## Pruning: magnitude, SparseGPT, Wanda
Magnitude pruning retains entries selected by weight magnitude; storage includes values plus sparse indices/pointers, so the nominal nonzero count is not the full byte cost. SparseGPT is a one-shot pruning method that uses second-order information; Wanda uses weight magnitude combined with activation magnitude for pruning decisions. Both target sparse weights rather than a fixed dense structured operator. Sparse layouts need index representation unless a structured sparse pattern is imposed. They are important equal-byte baselines: a sparsity percentage alone is not a byte-matched comparison, and any resulting gain is not evidence of rotor structure. Verified sources: \citep{frantar2023sparsegpt,sun2023wanda}.

## Geometric algebra and rotors
A rotor in Clifford/geometric algebra is an even multivector implementing a geometric rotation through the sandwich action on vectors; it is not automatically the same representation as a sequence of coordinate-plane Givens rotations used here. Geometric Clifford Algebra Networks provide a neural-network construction based on multivectors and geometric algebra operations. Their parameterization/count depends on algebra dimension, channels, and layer design. Algebraic products can avoid explicit coordinate-pair indices when conventions are fixed, but do not establish byte-efficient storage of arbitrary Transformer projection matrices. The relation worth investigating is whether a cascade can be interpreted as a restricted coordinate representation of geometric rotations, and whether the algebraic formulation adds useful structure beyond orthogonal constraints. Verified source: \citep{ruhe2023geometric}. The foundational reference for rotors in Clifford algebra is **To verify** before citation.

## Open questions
1. At equal *actual serialized bytes*, how does the cascade compare with truncated SVD, SVD--Givens, sparse, butterfly, and alternative structured matrices across FFN shapes and byte targets?
2. How do fitting objective (weight versus activation-weighted), topology, depth, and optional residual rank affect language-model and task quality?
3. Do deterministic pair schedules yield practical kernels with favorable memory traffic and latency, especially for decode-sized batches, or only attractive parameter counts?
4. How should low-rank residuals be allocated under a fixed byte budget without obscuring the full-rank cascade's contribution?
5. Which geometric-algebra rotor restrictions correspond to the implemented real coordinate-plane rotations, and do they offer an independent compression advantage?
6. What are the exact byte and parameter conventions for each comparison, including sparse index overhead, biases, dtype, and file metadata?
